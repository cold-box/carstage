# Audio rules

- Capture uses `MicCapture` (mono 16-bit, 48 kHz by default, 2048-sample frames). Analysis runs on the capture thread; audio never leaves to a file or the network.
- `SignalAnalysis`, `YinPitchDetector` and `OnsetDetector` are pure Kotlin with no Android dependency. When you change them, add tests to `app/src/test/.../SignalAnalysisTest.kt`.
- The hidden constant `AudioDeviceInfo.TYPE_ECHO_REFERENCE` cannot be referenced from the SDK, so the value 28 is written directly.
- The car's speakers reach the mic too. Never assume the voice is fully separated: the recorded singer's voice is measured along with the passengers', and only the real car's echo canceller can tell them apart (`docs/09`).
- `AudioAnalyzer` is the product's only sound analysis (`docs/09` sections 3 and 5).
    - Accompaniment (`Accompaniment`): per frequency bin, the lowest 20% of the last 1.5 s (36 frames) is the accompaniment, capped at the current frame. A note held for more than 1.2 s sinks into the accompaniment.
    - Loudness (`loudness`): sound standing out above the accompaniment in the voice band (250 Hz–4 kHz) over the whole accompaniment (dB), divided by 20 dB. It does not change with volume or mic gain; raising the volume reads loud for only about 1.2 s.
    - Cheer (`AudioEvent.Cheer`, decided by `CheerDetector`): the voice band is at least 12 dB above the accompaniment and at least 3 dB above the mean energy of the last ~5 s (the whole history for the first 5 s), and in 0.3 s (7 frames) at most 3 frames are pitched or chord-like (voice-band spectral flatness below 0.01). After a cheer it rests 4 s, and the first 1.5 s (while the accompaniment builds) never count. The pitch condition filters loud singing, the flatness condition filters piano chords (`docs/09` section 5: false cheers on three piano pieces went from 14 to 1, real cheer clips unchanged). Frames clearly above the accompaniment are pitch-checked even below -50 dBFS, so quiet in-car singing does not become a cheer. Known limits: cheers as loud as the singing, or a lone clear-pitched shout, can be missed; music jumping about 20 dB at once, noisy loud sounds in music (snare rolls, electronic effects) and piano chords near the flatness threshold (once in a nocturne) can read as cheers.
    - Mood (`AudioEvent.Mood`, decided by `MoodTracker`): every 2 s from the per-frame rise of five bands outside the voice band (40–250 Hz, 4–12 kHz; drum strength) and its autocorrelation over the last 6 s (beat regularity, 60–180 BPM), smoothed with a ~5 s time constant. After a song change the new mood appears in about 6–10 s. Drum strength ≥ 2.0 dB with regularity ≥ 0.35 is excited, below 1.4 dB is calm, otherwise normal; the previous mood holds until a threshold is clearly crossed. `energy` is where the accompaniment estimate sits in its last 30 s range. The drum thresholds sit between ten clean tracks; in-car recordings and conversation (all normal) only confirmed them (`docs/09` section 5 "곡 분위기 층"). An excited song through car speakers and a mic can come out normal.
    - Song end (`AudioEvent.SongEnded`, decided by `SongEndDetector`): after at least 30 s of sound (quiet frames excluded) since opening or the last end, a frame level 15 dB or more below the top-20% point of the accompaniment's last 30 s, held for 1 s. Being a guess from sound, it fires at most once per 30 s. 15 dB was chosen over 20, 12 and 10 dB on song sets with gaps, six full-length tracks and ambient tracks; recordings and conversation gave zero ends for every candidate (`docs/09` section 5 "곡 종료 감지"). Known limits: gapless albums, gaps shorter than 1 s, talk or cheers in the gap, car noise within 15 dB of the music, and songs ending within 30 s of opening are missed; quiet time before music starts counts toward the first song's 30 s; quiet ambient passages, stopped music and a big mid-song volume cut can read as ends. When the music app's playback info is available (notification access or the song list connection), `TrackEnds` ends the song at the track change, which removes most misses; a gap within 30 s after that is the same song, and a track change within 5 s after a sound-detected end or within 30 s after a manual end is the same end (`StageController`).
    - The cheer level thresholds (12 dB, 3 dB, 7 frames) are first values from the nature of the sound, not fitted to recordings. The flatness threshold 0.01 sits between measurements of piano pieces and real cheer clips. The pitch condition came from the team's internal test recordings; dropping the bass condition and the 3 dB recent-contrast came from comparing candidates on synthetic sounds mixed over them (`docs/09` section 5). When changing them, check with the per-condition tests (`CheerDetectorTest`), the synthetic-signal tests (`AudioAnalyzerTest`) and replays.
    - Frames below -80 dBFS are digital silence (the zeros Android gives when another app holds the mic) and are kept out of the accompaniment estimate, cheers, mood and song ends. Letting them in drags the accompaniment to the floor, and everything reads loud once sound returns.
    - When you change analysis or effect rules, run the unit tests (`AudioAnalyzerTest`, `CheerDetectorTest`, `MoodTrackerTest`, `SongEndDetectorTest`, `ScoreKeeperTest`, `GlowDirectorTest`, `LayeredDirectorTest`) and replay recordings (`scripts/replay.sh <file>`; the team's recordings and their expected answers are internal), then compare the log's `off after` line (duration, frames, sung frames, loud frames, cheers, silent frames and longest silence, peak, accompaniment level, mood counts and changes, last tempo, song ends heard, voice/song mode frames and switches). If you change voice-mode detection (`stage/VoiceMode`), also re-measure the voice-mode share with music alone (`docs/05` "목소리 모드 확인"). Frames far fewer than duration × 23 mean input stalled; many silent frames mean only zeros came in.
    - `silenced=true` on the mic start line, or a `microphone silenced by the system` warning mid-stage, means another app (Gleo, for example) took the mic or it was muted, and only zeros arrive.
    - The capture failure callback (`onFailure` of `MicCapture.start`) has no default. Every caller, the Lab included, must log it so the mic never stops silently.
- Whether music plays is decided from `AudioManager`'s playback state, never the mic (`MusicPlayback`: playing if the playback list has `USAGE_MEDIA`, speech `CONTENT_TYPE_SPEECH` excluded). The first screen's launch suggestion uses it. The mic turns on only after the user opens the stage and stops at once when a call mode starts.
    - Other apps' playback is visible without permission through `activePlaybackConfigurations` and `registerAudioPlaybackCallback` (usage `USAGE_MEDIA`). `isMusicActive()` stays `true` for a while after playback stops, so do not use it.
    - Calls are known from `AudioManager.mode` becoming `RINGTONE`/`IN_CALL`/`IN_COMMUNICATION`, caught at the moment of change with `addOnModeChangedListener`. Test on the emulator with `adb emu gsm call <number>`.

# Pleos policy

Sources: [performance and resource management](https://document.pleos.ai/docs/pleos-only/vehicle-app-planning-guide/performance-resource-mgmt), [audio focus development guide](https://document.pleos.ai/docs/pleos-only/vehicle-app-developer-guide/audio-focus-development-guide/audio-focus-development).

- While parked: CPU ≤ 25%, RAM ≤ 400 MB. When you change analysis code, check CPU on the emulator (`adb shell top -b -n 4 -d 3 -p $(adb shell pidof com.carstage.app)`, add `-H` per thread).
    - During a stage most CPU goes to screen updates (19–24% of one core, of which analysis and effect threads are 1–4%). Whether 25% means one core is still unknown.
- Effects request audio focus before playing. Over another app's music use transient focus with ducking, stop at once on focus loss (a Gleo call, for example), and give focus back in low-power ignition-off states.
    - Implementation: `audio/EffectPlayer`. Usage `USAGE_ASSISTANCE_SONIFICATION` plays alongside the music app without stopping it (`USAGE_MEDIA` and `USAGE_GAME` share the music group on Android Automotive, so the music app could lose focus). Focus is held for the sound's length plus 0.2 s, then released (`docs/05` "효과음 확인"). Convert new effect files with `scripts/sound-effect.sh`.
    - The MC voice (`audio/Announcer`, Pleos TextToSpeech SDK in `ON_DEVICE` mode) follows the same rules. The car's voice service (`ai.pleos.playground.caas`) produces the sound, and the app passes `USAGE_ASSISTANCE_SONIFICATION` and `CONTENT_TYPE_SPEECH` with `setAudioAttributes` (without it the voice plays as `USAGE_MEDIA` and music detection reads it as another app's music). While speaking it holds transient focus with the same usage, stops on loss, and `echoing()` feeds cheer discarding. Permission `pleos.car.permission.TTS_SERVICE`; the SDK adds the `<queries>` for the voice service. SDK calls catch exceptions; if the service does not start (5 s), returns an unrecoverable error (`PERMISSION_DENIED`, `UNAVAILABLE_LANGUAGE`, …) or does not start a line, the engine is dropped for that stage and the screen text stands alone. SDK events do not say which line they belong to, so they count for the line being spoken, and the `onStop` of the app's own `stop()` is counted and discarded. The engine is held only while the stage is open; a line ends after twice its expected length plus 1 s (≤ 8 s). While muted there is no engine and no length, so cheers are discarded for the expected length (130 ms per character plus 300 ms).
    - New effects use CC0 sources only, listed in README "효과음 출처". While the mic hears the app's own sound (`EffectPlayer.echoing`), cheer detection is discarded.
    - Pan and rate of a playing effect change through `EffectPlayer.change(SoundChange)` (per-stream partial volumes via `SoundPool.setVolume`, `setRate`). The driver is on the left. Only the duel announcement's drumroll and fanfare use it. Even when played faster, audio focus and the cheer discard window keep the original length.
    - Debug-build tests (`scripts/regress.sh`) mute only the sound through `EffectPlayer.muted`; lengths and `EchoWindow` stay as if sound played. When you change effect behavior, keep the muted path in step.

# Pleos emulator (v2.1.0) behavior

- Third-party apps can open MIC, VOICE_RECOGNITION, VOICE_COMMUNICATION, UNPROCESSED, CAMCORDER and VOICE_PERFORMANCE at 48 kHz, unmuted.
- No AEC/NS/AGC effects are available to apps (`isAvailable() == false`).
- The input devices include an Echo-Reference Mic (type 28), but recording from it is refused (system permission required).
- Media plays out through `bus0_media_out`. On the emulator playback does not return to the mic, so echo cancellation cannot be judged there; it needs a real car.
- The host mic turns on with `adb emu avd hostmicon` (`scripts/run-emulator.sh` does it). Off, recording still works but only the emulator's tiny noise arrives (at most ±7 LSB, about -73 dBFS). Allow qemu's mic access if macOS asks.
- Check the mic is live with `--es probe smoke`: a peak above -60 dBFS means host sound arrives. A quiet room with a MacBook's built-in mic gave a mean of -42 to -45 dBFS and peaks of -25 to -34 dBFS.
